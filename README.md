# Fourier Optics for Inference 01 — Fourier Optics for Engineers

How a lens computes a Fourier transform and a 4f system a convolution, in one pass of light: where the Fourier plane is, how the pattern scales with wavelength and why the light must be coherent; spatial light modulators, what a pixelated detector reads, and coherent (balanced) detection; on-chip slab lenses, butterfly meshes and convolution on a chip; a photonic link module's signal path and its slab lens; the precision and energy bill: ENOB, noise, passes, binary bit planes and the converters around the optics; what a transformer layer's linear algebra would ask of optics, and why a fixed band suits optics where top-k does not; and, to close, a warning not to confuse the chip's spatial transform with the fibre's bit stream. It opens with the optics vocabulary (plane wave, ray, wavefront, phase, spatial frequency, focal plane) for readers new to optics. Every concept has a figure, and every mechanism an animation driven by a tested model.

**26 slides** (00–25, in reading order: free-space physics, data in and out, on a chip, a real module, precision and energy, what it is for, and a closing warning), with a figure on every slide (**36 figures**, several multi-panel), **15 of them stepped animations** (play, pause, step, scrub, speed, reset; paused under reduced motion). The animations and figures are drawn from small model functions (an FFT, a tilted plane wave's ripple, paraxial ray transfer, path-length phasors, a four-port slab lens, convolution on a chip, balanced detection, an NRZ low-pass and eye, bit-plane arithmetic, a transformer layer's optical/electronic crossings), checked by a node test and in the browser. The fixed-band experiment numbers on slide 23 are read at build time from DeMo_Fixed_Band_Experiment at commit b1f62d3.

Topics: 4f system, Convolution theorem, Coherent detection, Photonic link module, Bit planes, ENOB, Conversion energy.
